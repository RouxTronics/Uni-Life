# Overview 
named after [Gordon Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Moore "Gordon Moore")

Moore’s law describes the trend that processing power doubles every 2 years, due to the doubling of transistors. 

One could argue that Moore’s law ended for a few reasons:

• as advancements in semiconductor fabrication slowed, there were smaller transistor count increments per year, 

• power and thermal limits set boundaries to how high the frequency can be increased and how many of the modules on the die can be powered simultaneously, 

• routine tasks have a limit to how “parallel” they can be, negating the effect of more cores.
