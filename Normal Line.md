In geometry and physics, a **normal line** (or simply the "normal") is an **imaginary reference line drawn perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to a surface** at the exact point where a ray or force meets that surface.


## Why Do We Use It?

Surfaces can be flat, curved, or tilted at different angles. Measuring light rays relative to a local $90^\circ$ normal line creates a consistent coordinate system, making laws like [[Snell's Law]] or the [[Law of Reflection]] work universally regardless of surface orientation.
